One engine, two directions

You’ve been computing free energy twice — once over your beliefs about now (perception, Part I), once over your actions into the future (choice, Part II). This module does one thing: it shows they are not two machines. They are one engine, run in two directions — and seeing that is seeing what a mind is.

Five doors; the plain line runs straight through — take whichever fit: ▶ Picture it, ✎ Try it (commit before the reveal), ✓ Check it yourself, ▸ Go deeper, ⬡ See it in UNI. Runs locally — no account, no network — adapts to light and dark; honours reduced motion.

The one fence that never moves — and in Part II it is stricter. “Perception,” “action,” “free energy” are functional quantities: one minimisation, pushed on either side of a prediction gap. For you we use felt words — a person reading a wind, easing a sheet. For UNI we use functional ones. Neither is awareness or will. Part I’s perception half rests on the proven, sealed Gate F0 arithmetic, but Part II’s action half — expected free energy — is not yet built or sealed in the organism; it needs its own lab-team verdict. So the unity here is standard active inference and the project’s design, but every claim about the organism is “built to,” never “does” — held open on its action half until the build proves it.

0 · From memory

First, the sailor in a gust — cold

No peeking. A sailor feels the boat heel harder than expected in a gust. Two ways to close that gap: (a) decide “this is a stiffer breeze than I read,” or (b) ease the sheet to spill wind.

1 · Side by side

Lay the two jobs down plainly

  • Perception (Part I). A gap between what you expect and what you sense. You close it by moving your belief until expectation meets sensation. Free energy, minimised over beliefs, about now.
  • Action (Part II). A gap between what you expect (what you’re built to want) and what you sense. You close it by moving the world until sensation meets expectation. Free energy, minimised over actions, over the future.
✎ Try it — same ____, different ____ commit first
Stare at those two for a second and find what’s identical and what’s different. Commit your answer before reading on: same ____, different ____. (The next section is the whole lesson in that one blank-filling.)
2 · Same gap, opposite side

One valley, one ball — two ways down

Here it is. Both close the very same gap — expectation against sensation — and both do it by minimising the very same quantity, free energy. The only difference is which side they push: perception moves the belief to meet the world (bend the map to the territory); action moves the world to meet the belief (bend the territory to the map).

Close the gap — from either side

The dot is your belief; the line is the world you sense; the shaded band between them is the gap (free energy). Two controls: lower the belief to the world (perceive), or raise the world to the belief (act). Watch the question "are these two different machines?" fall apart — either control lowers the same gap. A reveal of one-engine-two-directions, not an animated curve.

● belief (what you expect)▬ world (what you sense)
A gap: belief up here, world down there. Close it — push either side. Which engine is it when you do?
▶ Picture it — one valley, one ball kid
One valley, one ball. Sometimes the ball rolls because you let your belief slide to the floor (you realise it’s just morning haze, not a front). Sometimes it rolls because you change the world so the floor comes up to the ball (you light the fire, and now the room really is warm). Same valley, same downhill — the mind just chooses which thing to move.
✓ Check it yourself — both at once, right now no calculator
Take any waking second and you’ll find both running at once: reading these words, your eyes act (they flick to the next word — changing your sensation on purpose) while your mind perceives (updating what the sentence means). Perception and action aren’t taking turns; they’re one loop, pushing both sides of the same gap continuously. Catch yourself doing it, right now.
▸ Go deeper — one objective, two arguments derivation
It is literally one objective with two free arguments. Perception minimises free energy over the belief q (Part I); action minimises it over the actions that shape future sensations (Part II). Same functional, two things you’re allowed to vary — update q, or change the data by acting — whichever lowers it more. (The exact forward form, G = risk + ambiguity, is Module 14; here the point is only that it is the same quantity.)
3 · What this unifies

Many things, one thing

Stand back and see how much just collapsed into one:

  • Perceiving the world and acting on it — one engine.
  • Believing (a guess chasing truth) and wanting (a prediction you won’t surrender) — the same beliefs, used two ways.
  • Explaining what is and choosing what to do — minimising free energy now, and expected free energy next.

Everything a mind does — seeing, inferring, wanting, moving, exploring — is one quantity being pushed downhill, on whichever side of the gap gives. That is the single sentence the whole book has been building toward, and you can now say it yourself:

A mind is a thing that minimises its free energy, by changing its beliefs or its world, whichever is cheaper.
⬡ See it in UNI — held open design · action half not sealed
This unity is exactly what the organism is being built to be: one blanket minimising one free energy, free to update its beliefs (perceive) or drive its motors (act) on the same gap. The perception side rests on Part I’s sealed Gate F0 arithmetic; the action side — expected free energy — is not yet built or sealed in DDNA and needs its own lab-team verdict. So the unity is the design and the standard theory, held open on its action half until the build proves it. “Built to be,” never “is.”
4 · Where this goes next

A future that branches

The keystone is set: perception and action are one engine, two directions, minimising one free energy. You can read a whole mind in that one line now.

But we’ve quietly kept the choice small — “sweater or fire,” two options, one step. A living mind acts into a future that branches: this, then that, then the next, each step changing what it will know and so what it should do next. Choosing a whole path through an uncertain, branching future raises a problem a single step never had — and how a mind handles it is Module 13.

⬡ Notice what you just did self-reference
You held Part I in one hand and Part II in the other, looked for the seam between them, and found there wasn’t one. A great deal of machinery turned out to be a single idea seen from two sides. That collapse — many things becoming one — is what understanding feels like, and you just did it to the subject of the whole book.
