Start the second engine

Part I was a mind making its beliefs fit the world — perception, rolling free energy downhill by re-believing. Part II is the other direction: a mind making the world fit its beliefs. That is action, the whole of behaviour — and it is not a new engine. It is the same free energy, pointed the other way.

Five doors; the plain line runs straight through — take whichever fit: ▶ Picture it, ✎ Try it (commit before the reveal), ✓ Check it yourself, ▸ Go deeper, ⬡ See it in UNI. Runs locally — no account, no network — adapts to light and dark; honours reduced motion.

The one fence that never moves — and in Part II it is stricter. “Free energy,” “belief,” “goal,” “want” are functional quantities: a model’s predictions and the drives to close their gaps. For you we use felt words — you are a person pulling on a sweater. For UNI we use functional ones: a drive in a model reaching to change its sensors’ input. Neither is awareness, feeling, desire, or sentience. Part I’s free energy was proven and sealed (Gate F0). Part II’s action machinery — expected free energy — is NOT yet built or sealed in the organism; a new free-energy term needs its own lab-team verdict. So the math here is standard active inference you can compute, but every claim about the organism is “built to,” never “does” — held open until its gate proves it.

1 · A gap in the body

A gap you can feel

Forget numbers for a moment. You’re cold. Not dangerously — that ordinary, nagging cold where part of you keeps insisting things should be warmer. Sit with what that is, in the language you already own: part of you expects to be warm, and you’re sensing cold. Expectation against sensation — a gap, free energy, running high. You have two engines for it. Try them in turn.

▶ Picture it — water and bedrock kid
Perception is water finding its level — beliefs flowing until they match the ground. But some expectations aren’t water; they’re bedrock. “Stay warm,” “stay fed,” “stay whole.” Pour all the re-believing you like over bedrock and it just runs off. The gap stays — so the other engine has to move.
2–3 · Two engines, one gap

Perception bounces off — so you act

Try the first engine honestly: right now, try to believe your way out of being cold — to genuinely expect cold and stop feeling that it’s wrong. You can’t. “I should be warm” isn’t a casual belief you picked up; it’s one of those expectations you cannot talk yourself out of, written in deeper than experience — the un-erasable priors from the dark room. The re-believing engine just bounces off. So you reach for a sweater. The sensation changes; the gap closes; free energy drops — and your belief never moved an inch. The world moved to meet it.

Which engine closes the gap?

✓ Check it yourself — belief or world? one-line test
Here’s how to tell perception from action in any moment: did the belief move, or did the world? You smell smoke and decide “the house is on fire” — belief moved: perception. You smell smoke and open a window — world moved: action. Run three from your last hour and sort them. Every single thing a mind does is one or the other — closing the same gap from opposite sides.
▸ Go deeper — one free energy, two ways to lower it derivation
Free energy is a gap between what a model predicts and what it gets. There are exactly two variables you can change to shrink it: the belief (perception — minimise over q, Part I) or the sensations (action — change the world so the incoming data moves). Same objective, two arguments. An un-erasable prior is one with such high precision that re-believing barely moves the gap — so the only term left to minimise through is action. That is why bedrock priors are the engine of behaviour, not an obstacle to it.
4 · The reversal

A prediction becomes a goal

In perception, your belief is a guess that chases the truth — it’s trying to be correct. But watch what your belief did when you put on the sweater. It wasn’t chasing the truth; the truth (you’re cold) was plain. Your belief refused to update and became the thing the world had to be made to match. Your prediction (“warm”) stopped being a guess and became a goal.

A creature’s goals are just its predictions it refuses to give up.

Perception makes beliefs fit the world; action makes the world fit beliefs. And the beliefs it fights for — the ones it would rather rebuild the world than surrender — are exactly the un-erasable priors from the dark room. Those priors aren’t only what keep a mind out of the dark room; they are the engine of everything it does. Want is just an expectation you won’t let go of, reaching for the world.

⬡ See it in UNI — held open design · not a sealed capability
The organism is being built so that the expectations it is born carrying (its genome’s priors) would drive it to act on its world, not only to re-believe it — the same two engines, closing the same gap. In functional terms: a drive written into a model, never a want or a feeling. Honesty gate. Unlike Part I’s free energy, sealed at Gate F0, the action machinery — expected free energy — is not yet built or sealed in the organism; a new free-energy term needs its own lab-team verdict. This box describes the design and the standard theory, held open — it is not a claim that the organism acts, chooses, wants, or feels anything today. “Built to,” never “does.”
5 · Where this goes next

Choosing a future you haven’t lived

You’ve started the second engine. Action lowers free energy by changing the world instead of the mind; it’s forced by the priors you can’t re-believe; and in it a prediction becomes a goal. But a cold creature doesn’t have one way to get warm — a sweater, a fire, movement, another body to huddle against. The moment there’s more than one action, a new question lands:

Before you’ve done any of them — before you’ve felt which works — how do you choose? What makes one action better than another, when the future it leads to hasn’t happened yet?

To choose, a mind has to score a future it hasn’t lived. That scoring has a name, and it is the forward cousin of everything you built in Part I. It is Module 10.
