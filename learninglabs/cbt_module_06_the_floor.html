1 · The same height, split a different way

Free energy = surprise + gap

In Module 4 you built free energy from the joint: F = Σ q(η)[ln q(η) − ln p(y,η)] — perfect for computing, no evidence in it. The same number rearranges to show something the first form hid, because the joint splits: p(y,η) = p(η|y)·p(y) (posterior × evidence). Out drops:

F = −ln p(y) + KL(q ‖ posterior)

In plain words: free energy = the surprise of what happened + how far your belief is from the true one. The evidence p(y) reappears — but as a fixed constant (it doesn’t depend on your belief b). You still never computed it to use F; it only shows up when we ask what F is made of.

▸ Go deeper — the rearrangement, by hand derivation 
F = Σ q(η)[ln q(η) − ln p(y,η)]
  = Σ q(η)[ln q(η) − ln p(η|y) − ln p(y)]     (split the joint)
  = Σ q(η) ln( q(η)/p(η|y) )  −  ln p(y)·Σ q(η)
  = KL( q ‖ posterior )  +  ( −ln p(y) )
So F = −ln p(y) + KL(q ‖ p(η|y)) — the surprise plus a gap that is the distance from your belief to the true posterior.
2 · The gap can’t be negative · 3 · Why · 4 · In the numbers

There is a floor, and you cannot cross it

Everything hinges on that second piece, the gap = KL(q ‖ posterior), and one fact finishes the spine: the gap is never negative — zero only when your belief is the posterior, positive for every other belief. Since F = surprise + gap and gap ≥ 0:

F ≥ −ln p(y) = 0.3710636814

The valley has a floor — the surprise — and no belief, however clever, can push free energy beneath it. Try it: set any belief and watch F sit on or above the floor, never below.

▶ Picture it — the proof, one line above a curve kid · the why
Draw y = ln(x), then the straight line y = x − 1. They touch at exactly one spot, x = 1, and everywhere else the line sits above the curve: ln(x) ≤ x − 1, equality only at 1. Apply it inside the gap with x = p(η|y)/q(η), sum up, and the “≤ x−1” pieces cancel to zero — leaving the gap ≥ 0, equality only when your belief is the posterior. That’s Gibbs’ inequality, and the floor is forced by the shape of the logarithm.
✓ Check it yourself — the gap in numbers equation · by hand
At b = 0.5: gap = F − surprise = 0.944576 − 0.371064 = 0.573512 — and KL(0.5‖0.913) = 0.5·ln(0.5/0.913) + 0.5·ln(0.5/0.087) = 0.573512, matching exactly. At b = 0.913: gap = 0.371064 − 0.371064 = 0 — the belief is the posterior, so it sits on the floor. Every other belief sits strictly above.
▸ Go deeper — Gibbs, and the bound tags derivation
−gap = Σ q(η) ln(p(η|y)/q(η)) ≤ Σ q(η)[p(η|y)/q(η) − 1] = Σ p(η|y) − Σ q(η) = 1 − 1 = 0, so gap ≥ 0, equality iff q is the posterior. This is the exact-case bound; F carries the tag :exact. Looser tags (:laplace, :sampled, :mmp) are ceilings for harder worlds — the :mmp form can even dip below the surprisal, so it is never used as a bound or fed back as a belief (the discipline MMP-BOUND-01). That is why every F wears a tag.

5 · Where this goes next

The punchline is set up

Free energy wears two faces: energy − entropy (computable, no evidence) and surprise + gap (which reveals the floor, F ≥ −ln p(y), touched only at the posterior) — and you proved the floor by hand with a tangent line. So the punchline is set: if the lowest free energy equals the surprise and is reached at the posterior, then minimizing the thing you can compute hands you back both the thing you couldn’t compute (the surprise) and the belief you couldn’t reach (Bayes), at once. Making that exact, with the “tag with teeth” that keeps it honest, is Module 7.

⬡ Notice what you just did self-reference
You took one number, split it a way that made a floor appear, then proved the floor couldn’t be crossed with a single line above a curve — no authority, your own tangent. A floor you can draw is a floor you can trust. That is the difference between being told the theory and owning it.
