1 · The move every mind makes

Carry a belief you can move

You can’t reach the true belief about the hidden cause — that needs the forbidden sum. So don’t try. Carry a stand-in belief instead: a guess you’re allowed to pick, hold, and slide around until it’s as good as you can make it.

Back in your doorway’s two-cause world from Module 2 — front on or off. The truth about how much to believe “on,” given the sky, was 0.913 — but that came from dividing by the evidence 0.69, the sum you’re avoiding. So set it aside. Instead, just pick a number for how strongly you’ll believe the front is on — and get ready to move it.

▶ Picture it — the dial kid
A single dial on the wall, marked off at the far left and on at the far right. Where you point it is your current belief about the hidden cause. You can turn it freely. Nobody handed you the right setting — the whole game from here is learning which way to turn it.
2 · What a belief now is

A belief is a position

So look at what a belief actually is now: not a fact, not a number handed down — a position. A spot on a line from 0 to 1.

(a) 0 = “certain the front is off.” (b) 0.5 = “genuinely torn — could be either.” (c) 1 = “certain the front is on.” Everything between is a shade of leaning. That one number is your whole belief about the hidden cause, and you can put it anywhere.

▸ Go deeper — the movable belief q(η) derivation
The movable belief is a distribution q(η) over the hidden cause η. In a two-cause world it’s pinned by one number — b = q(η = on) ∈ [0, 1], with q(η = off) = 1 − b. This q is the thing a mind actually carries and updates; it is not the true posterior p(η | y) (that needs the evidence). q is a free stand-in we get to choose — well, without ever computing the evidence.

That’s the earned idea: a belief is a position b on [0, 1] you can move. Now — how do you tell a good position from a bad one, when you can’t peek at the true answer?

3 · Three pressures you can feel

Fit, stretch, looseness

You can’t score a position against the truth (behind the wall). But you can feel three honest pressures on it, each judged from things in your hands. Meet them one at a time — agreeing case first.

  • Fit. Does this position explain the sky you saw? The wet sky is predicted well by “on” (0.9), badly by “off” (0.2). Fit pulls the dial toward whatever explains your senses.
  • Stretch. How far did you drag the dial from where you started — your prior, 0.7? Abandoning a lifetime of mornings on a whim is expensive. Stretch pulls the dial back toward your prior.
  • Looseness. How confident is the position — and has it earned that? A dial slammed to 0.99 claims near-certainty; one near 0.5 stays humble. Looseness pulls the dial toward the honest middle.

No single position wins all three. Fit wants the bold 0.95, Stretch wants the prior 0.7, Looseness wants the humble 0.5 — three different spots. Trust fit alone → the gambler who’s always certain; stretch alone → the stubborn prior that never learns; looseness alone → the eternal shrug. A good belief balances all three — which forces putting them on one scale and adding them up. That single number is Module 4.

4 · Where this goes next

The score is forced

You’ve made the first move around the wall. A belief is no longer a fact you can’t reach — it’s a position you can hold and slide, and you can feel three honest pressures on it: fit, stretch, looseness. And you proved, by your own ranking, that they pull in different directions — no position is best on all three at once.

So the next rung is forced: put the three pressures on one scale and add them into a single number — a height that’s low where a belief is good and high where it’s bad. That single number is the one this whole book is named for, and here’s the beautiful part waiting: it will contain no evidence term at all. The forbidden sum won’t appear. That’s how a mind scores its beliefs without ever walking through the wall. You assemble it in Module 4.

⬡ Notice what you just did self-reference
You couldn’t reach the true belief, so you did exactly what a mind does: carried a movable one, and judged it by pressures you can feel instead of a truth you can’t — and found, by your own hand, that no single pressure is enough. That tension is why free energy has the shape it has, and you felt the need for it before you were handed it.
