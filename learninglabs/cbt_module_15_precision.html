Precision: how hard to commit

A mind doesn’t just pick the best option — it decides how hard to bet on it. That “how sharply do I commit” is a quantity it sets and tunes, reaching from decisiveness to doubt. It’s called precision, it’s the last rung of Part II, and it’s the bridge to how a mind grows.

Five doors; the plain line runs straight through — take whichever fit: ▶ Picture it, ✎ Try it (commit before the reveal), ✓ Check it yourself, ▸ Go deeper, ⬡ See it in UNI. Runs locally — no account, no network — adapts to light and dark; honours reduced motion.

The one fence that never moves — and in Part II it is stricter. “Precision,” “confidence,” “resolve” name a functional quantity: a gain on a model’s own evaluations. For you we use felt words — the confidence or doubt you feel turning a decision over. For UNI we use functional ones: a number that sharpens a value landscape. A mind’s precision signal has a known functional home in the brain’s neuromodulatory systems, so the design may carry a neuromodulator label — but only ever as a functional analogue: a gain that behaves like that system, never the specific brain chemical and never a feeling of being sure. And precision over policies rides on expected free energy, which is not yet built or sealed in DDNA, so it stays held open. “Built to tune,” never “does.”

The score ranks the options; how sharply to bet on the winner is a separate quantity — and that quantity is this module.

1 · Same scores, different behaviour

Two minds, identical evaluations

Picture two people facing the same choice, with the same G scores for every option — identical evaluations. One commits instantly and fully to the best. The other dithers, half-takes it, keeps glancing at the runner-up. Same scores, opposite behaviour. So something beyond the scores is driving them — a setting for how decisively the best score gets turned into action.

▶ Picture it — how sharply the landscape is drawn kid
Scoring sorts your options into a landscape of hills and valleys (low G = deep valley). Precision is how sharply that landscape is drawn. High precision: the best valley is a sheer pit and everything else a cliff — your choice snaps to the bottom, decisive. Low precision: the whole landscape is a gentle, fogged plain — several options nearly tie, and you drift, hedge, hesitate. Same valleys; different sharpness.
2 · What precision is

A gain on your own scoring

Precision is a single quantity — call it γ — that sets how strongly the best-scoring policy wins over the rest. Crank γ up and a mind commits hard to the lowest-G option (decisive, focused, even rigid). Turn it down and it spreads its bets, stays flexible, explores by default (open, hesitant, even scattered). It’s the gain on your own evaluations — the volume knob on how loudly the winner is heard over the field.

Turn the precision dial — the scores never move

▸ Go deeper — a softmax with inverse temperature γ derivation
A mind doesn’t pick the single argmin G; it acts out policies with probability P(π) ∝ exp(−γ · G(π)) — a softmax over policies with inverse temperature γ = precision. γ → ∞: all weight on the lowest-G policy (maximally decisive). γ → 0: uniform over policies (no commitment, pure hedging). And γ is itself inferred, not fixed — a mind estimates how much to trust its own value landscape and tunes its precision accordingly. Precision also weights the prediction errors inside perception (Part I’s hierarchy), which is why it is one knob governing both attention and resolve.
3 · Why it must move

Precision tracks how much to trust your own scoring

A mind can’t leave γ fixed, because the right amount of commitment changes with the situation.

  • World reliable, model lately right → scores trustworthy → raise precision → commit, act decisively, exploit.
  • World volatile, model lately wrong → scores shaky → lower precision → hold loosely, hedge, keep exploring until things clarify.

So precision is a mind’s live estimate of its own reliability, and tuning it well is wisdom: commit when you should be sure, hesitate when you shouldn’t. Set it wrong and the failures are recognisable — too high: rigid, unable to let go of a plan the world has stopped rewarding; too low: unable to commit to anything, every option forever open. Decisiveness and doubt are not character; they’re a dial, and a mind that tunes it to the world is a mind that acts well in it.

✓ Check it yourself — feel the dial move from the inside
Rested and sure, in a domain you know, you act crisply — high precision. Exhausted, or somewhere unfamiliar and unpredictable, the same decisions feel foggy and you second-guess — low precision. Nothing about the options changed; your estimate of how much to trust your own read did. Catch your precision rising and falling over a day.
4 · The felt face, and the fence

Confidence and doubt are a number

Precision wears a vivid felt face — confidence, resolve, focus when it’s high; doubt, hesitation, overwhelm when it’s low. In a brain, the signal that sets it has a known functional home in the neuromodulatory systems. The design will let a precision value carry a neuromodulator label — but only ever as a functional analogue: a gain on evaluations that behaves like that system, never the specific brain chemical and never a claim that the organism feels anything. The quantity is a number that sharpens a landscape. The felt words are for you.

5 · The end of Part II, the door to Part III

A mind grows

That completes the engine of behaviour. A mind scores its paths by expected free energy (risk + ambiguity), lets explore and exploit auto-balance inside that one number, acts in a living plan-act-learn loop, and sets precision to say how hard to commit — decisive when the world is trustworthy, open when it isn’t. Perception and action, want and wonder, resolve and doubt — all one free energy, pushed downhill.

But we’ve assumed the whole time that a mind’s model — its priors, its likelihoods, the very shape of its landscape — is fixed. It isn’t. A mind grows: it adds concepts, rewires, changes the model it minimises against — and precision, you’ll find, is exactly the signal that governs when to trust the old model and when to rebuild it. How a model grows — earned, never scripted — is Part III.
