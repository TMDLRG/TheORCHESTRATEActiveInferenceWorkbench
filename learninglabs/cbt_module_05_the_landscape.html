Free energy over every belief makes a shape — a valley. Its slope is a log-odds mismatch you can feel blind; a ball dropped anywhere rolls to the same floor. And the belief at that floor is the one you couldn’t compute — the posterior. The valley bottom is Bayes, reached without the evidence.

▸ Go deeper — the slope is a log-odds mismatch derivation
dF/db = ln(b/(1−b)) − ln(p(y,on)/p(y,off)) = ln(b/(1−b)) − ln(10.5) — your belief’s log-odds minus the joint’s log-odds, using only belief and joint (no evidence). It is zero exactly when b/(1−b) = 10.5, i.e. b = 0.913. “Slope = log-odds mismatch”: how far your belief’s confidence sits from what the joint alone recommends.

4 · Where this goes next

How low is the floor?

You drew the landscape and read everything off it: free energy is a valley over all beliefs; its slope is a log-odds mismatch you can feel blind; rolling downhill is inference; and the bottom is the posterior — Bayes, recovered without the evidence. One question is left, the sharpest: you found where the bottom is — but how low is it? What is the actual height at the floor, and could a cleverer belief ever dig below it? The answer is a hard floor with a famous name that ties this whole book back to Module 1’s very first number. That’s Module 6: the floor you cannot cross.
